5 Mistakes Companies Make With Office Cleaning

Why your Toronto, Mississauga, Brampton or Etobicoke office isn't as clean as it should be — and what you're losing because of it.

📖 15 min read · 📅 July 15, 2025 · ✍️ Shining Legacy

Introduction: The Hidden Cost of Poor Office Cleaning

A clean office isn't just about appearances. Studies show that cleanliness directly impacts employee productivity by up to 15%, reduces sick days by 25%, and influences how clients perceive your company within 30 seconds of walking in the door.

Yet companies across Toronto, Mississauga, Brampton, Etobicoke, Vaughan and Durham Region are making five critical mistakes that undermine their office environment — costing them an estimated $15,000+ yearly per facility in lost productivity, employee sick days, and damaged client perception.

If your office cleaning isn't meeting professional standards, one of these mistakes is probably why. And fixing it could transform your workplace.

Mistake #1: Cleaning Only Common Areas (Not Where Germs Really Live)

What most companies do: Hire cleaning staff to vacuum carpets, wipe desks, and clean bathrooms once daily or three times weekly.

Why it fails: 80% of workplace germs live on high-touch surfaces that are routinely ignored. Here's what typically gets missed:

  • Light switches & door handles
  • Elevator buttons & handrails
  • Phone handsets, keyboards & computer mice
  • Coffee machine handles & printer touchscreens
  • Shared equipment (staplers, remotes, pens)
  • Desk surfaces (only the obvious areas)

Studies show offices with shared equipment see 3x more sick days than offices with proper disinfection protocols. During cold and flu season, one contaminated doorknob can infect an entire floor within 48 hours.

What professional cleaning companies do instead:

  • ✓ Daily disinfection of ALL high-touch surfaces (not weekly)
  • ✓ WHMIS-compliant products killing 99.9% of germs in 10 seconds
  • ✓ Restroom deep-cleaning 3–5 times daily (not just once)
  • ✓ Keyboard/mouse/phone sanitization (usually completely missed by standard cleaners)
  • ✓ Documented tracking of which surfaces were disinfected

Mistake #2: Using One-Size-Fits-All Cleaning (Not Customized to Your Space)

Here's what we see constantly in Mississauga, Brampton and Etobicoke offices: cleaning companies arrive with identical procedures for a 5,000 sq ft law office and a 20,000 sq ft manufacturing facility. Different spaces need fundamentally different approaches.

Real-world examples of why one-size-fits-all fails:

  • Medical/Healthcare offices: Need infection-control protocols and compliance documentation. Standard office cleaning won't meet regulatory standards and puts patients at risk.
  • Retail/Customer-facing: Need morning deep-cleans BEFORE doors open. Evening-only cleaning leaves debris, dust and tracked-in dirt visible to customers all day.
  • Industrial/Warehouse: Need specialized floor cleaning equipment, dust control systems, safety hazard removal (not standard vacuum & mop).
  • Multi-tenant buildings: Need staggered timing so cleaning doesn't disrupt neighboring tenants. Shared spaces need different protocols than private offices.

What professionals do instead: Conduct a customized cleaning audit including:

  • ✓ Site-specific protocols (not generic checklists)
  • ✓ Timing optimized for YOUR business operations
  • ✓ Compliance documentation for regulated industries
  • ✓ Regular performance reviews (weekly quality audits with photos)

Mistake #3: Hiring Based on Price Alone (The Hidden Cost of "Cheap")

When you pick the cheapest cleaning quote, here's what typically happens:

  • Staff turnover is high — you get a different cleaner every 2–3 weeks
  • They're rushing through (cheap contracts = minimal time allocated)
  • Product quality is low — budget cleaners don't use WHMIS-compliant products that actually kill germs
  • No accountability — complaints go ignored or get passed along
  • Insurance & bonding are weak or non-existent (huge liability risk)

Real case study from Toronto: One downtown office building switched from a budget cleaner to professional cleaning. Results within 30 days:

  • Employee sick days down 18% (directly attributed to cleaner environment)
  • Client feedback improved ("Office looks incredible now")
  • Zero "missed spot" complaints
  • Monthly cost actually LOWER than the budget option (when accounting for productivity gains and no issues)

How to choose based on VALUE, not price:

  • ✓ Verify full insurance & bonding (fully insured is non-negotiable)
  • ✓ Check references from 3+ similar businesses
  • ✓ Ask about staff training standards & retention rates
  • ✓ Request a performance guarantee in writing
  • ✓ Demand they provide WHMIS-certified products

Mistake #4: Ignoring Carpet & Upholstery (The Bacteria Time Bomb)

Carpets look clean but harbour bacteria, dust mites, allergens and microscopic debris. Standard vacuuming removes only surface dust — not what's embedded deep within the fibres.

The facts about dirty carpet:

  • Carpets contain 200,000+ bacteria per square inch (dirtier than a toilet seat)
  • Dust mite populations triple in poorly maintained carpet
  • Airborne allergen levels increase dramatically, affecting employee respiratory health
  • Mold growth accelerates in damp carpet (common in Ontario humidity)

Most offices in the GTA only vacuum daily but NEVER deep-clean carpets. This is a serious health hazard and directly impacts sick days.

Professional carpet care schedule:

  • ✓ High-traffic areas: quarterly steam cleaning (deep extraction)
  • ✓ Regular-use areas: semi-annually
  • ✓ Upholstered furniture/chairs: annually (or more if heavily used)
  • ✓ Use WHMIS-compliant products (zero toxic residue, safe for occupants)

Mistake #5: Not Measuring & Tracking Cleaning Quality (Flying Blind)

Most office managers have NO way to measure if cleaning is actually happening at professional standards. They rely on "it looks okay" — which isn't a metric.

Without measurable standards, you don't know:

  • If cleaning is improving employee experience & health
  • Which areas need more attention or different products
  • If your cleaning company is worth what you're paying
  • If reported problems are actually getting fixed

How professional companies maintain accountability:

  • ✓ Weekly photo documentation (before/after for key areas)
  • ✓ Signed detailed quality checklist from cleaning team daily
  • ✓ Employee satisfaction surveys (monthly via simple form)
  • ✓ Performance review calls with cleaning company (bi-weekly minimum)
